The rapid growth of data centers poses significant environmental and social challenges that cannot be overlooked. These facilities consume immense amounts of energy, often sourced from fossil fuels, exacerbating climate change and straining local power grids. Additionally, the construction of data centers can disrupt ecosystems and contribute to habitat loss, which ultimately impacts wildlife and biodiversity in our communities.

Communities that have seen the influx of data centers often report increased traffic, noise pollution, and a drain on local resources. The promise of job creation is frequently outweighed by the long-term environmental costs and the feeling of being forgotten in favor of corporate profits. The potential for these facilities to monopolize land space can also hinder responsible urban planning and development, limiting opportunities for more sustainable projects that better serve local needs.

It is critical to prioritize our environment and community well-being over short-term economic gains. By preventing the construction of data centers in our state, we can better focus on sustainable development and renewable energy initiatives. Advocating for smart growth strategies will foster healthier communities and a more sustainable future for all residents, ensuring that our state remains a thriving place to live and work.
