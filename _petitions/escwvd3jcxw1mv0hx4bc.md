The accuracy of the U.S. Census plays a vital role in ensuring fair representation in Congress. Currently, the Census does not differentiate between U.S. citizens and non-citizens, leading to distortions in population data. This lack of clarity affects legislative districting and resource allocation, as areas with a high number of non-citizens might receive disproportionate representation and funding based on inflated population counts that do not reflect the actual voting populace.

 

Separating citizens from non-citizens in the Census would provide a clearer understanding of the demographic landscape in our country. It would enable lawmakers to tailor policies and services that directly address the needs of those who have the legal right to vote and participate in the democratic process. Accurate data is essential for drawing congressional districts in a manner that reflects the voice of American citizens.

 

Implementing this change would help uphold the principles of democracy by ensuring that each congressional district represents its voting population accurately. By advocating for this separation in Census data, we can work toward a more equitable and representative government that reflects the true demographics of our nation. It is time to prioritize the voices of citizens in our legislative processes.
