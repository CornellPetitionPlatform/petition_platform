The increasing number of data centers in our state poses serious environmental and social challenges. These facilities consume vast amounts of energy, often sourced from non-renewable resources, contributing to carbon emissions and climate change. As communities strive to transition to sustainable practices, the proliferation of energy-intensive data centers contradicts our collective goals for a greener future.

Additionally, the construction of these centers often leads to the degradation of local ecosystems and habitats. The demand for land to accommodate data centers can result in deforestation and the disruption of wildlife. Protecting our natural spaces is crucial not only for biodiversity but also for the quality of life in our communities, where access to green areas is essential for health and well-being.

To safeguard our environment and foster a sustainable future, it is imperative to halt the development of new data centers within our state and surrounding areas. By prioritizing renewable energy and investing in ecological conservation, we can better align our state's policies with the urgent need for climate action and environmental protection.
