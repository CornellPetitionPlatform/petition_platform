The fish counters installed along the Anchor Point River have become a significant threat to the local salmon population, raising urgent concerns among environmentalists and community members alike. Each year, these devices, designed for monitoring fish migration, inadvertently trap and kill countless salmon, disrupting their natural life cycle. The consequences extend beyond the ecosystem, jeopardizing the livelihood of those who rely on fishing and tourism in the area.

It is crucial to recognize that the salmon are vital not only for their ecological role but also for the cultural and economic well-being of our communities. Protecting this species is essential for maintaining biodiversity and ensuring sustainable fishing practices. Addressing the harm caused by these fish counters is not just an ecological issue but a moral one, as these devices continue to harm a cherished resource.

Action is necessary to remove or redesign these harmful fish counters to protect the salmon population and preserve our river's health. Local authorities and stakeholders must work together to find humane and effective alternatives that ensure both monitoring efforts and the survival of our vital salmon species. It’s time for our community to advocate for changes that respect both nature and the economic opportunities that come with it.
