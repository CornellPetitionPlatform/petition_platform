Fundraising in schools has increasingly placed an undue burden on students and their families. Children should be focusing on learning and personal development, not selling products or begging for donations. This constant emphasis on fundraising can create an unhealthy competitive environment, contributing to stress and anxiety among students who may feel pressured to participate or reach specific goals.

Moreover, these fundraisers often amplify inequality within the school community, as some families may be more equipped to sell or contribute than others. Public education should be accessible to all, regardless of financial resources, and reliance on fundraisers can create divisions that undermine the sense of community in our schools. When education is tied to fundraising success, it sends a message that some students are worth more than others based on their ability to generate funds.

It is crucial that schools focus on utilizing public funding to support education rather than relying on extra financial initiatives. Public money should be allocated effectively to ensure every student has access to essential resources and activities. Ending the practice of fundraisers will foster a more inclusive and supportive educational environment, where every child can thrive without the unnecessary pressure of fundraising demands.
