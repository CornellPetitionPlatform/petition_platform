The right to repair is essential for promoting sustainability, innovation, and consumer freedom. Current restrictions imposed by manufacturers hinder individuals from repairing their devices, leading to increased electronic waste and greater environmental harm. Making right to repair compliance mandatory would empower consumers to make informed choices and extend the lifecycle of products, significantly reducing the carbon footprint associated with manufacturing new items.

Mandatory right to repair compliance would also foster a competitive marketplace where independent repair shops could thrive. It would lower repair costs for consumers and encourage businesses to ensure their products are designed with longevity in mind. This shift would not only benefit consumers economically but also stimulate local economies through job creation in repair services.

Finally, enacting this legislation would encourage transparency in product design, pushing manufacturers to produce more durable goods with accessible replacement parts. The right to repair should not be a privilege but a fundamental consumer right that aligns with our collective goals of sustainability and responsible consumption.
